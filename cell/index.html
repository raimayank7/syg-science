The cell: unit of life

What a cell is, its main parts, and how plant cells differ from animal cells.

Classes 6 to 8Biology411 words

A building is made of bricks. In the same way, the body of every living thing is made of tiny units called cells. The cell is the basic unit of structure and function in all living things: it is the smallest part that is itself alive.

Discovery

In 1665 the English scientist Robert Hooke looked at a thin slice of cork under a microscope he had built. He saw rows of tiny empty boxes, like the cells of a honeycomb, and named them "cells". Most cells are far too small to be seen without a microscope.

One cell or many

Some living things are made of just one cell. They are called unicellular: Amoeba, Paramecium and bacteria are examples. That single cell does everything: it feeds, breathes, moves and reproduces. Plants, animals and human beings are multicellular. A human body has many trillions of cells.

Cells come in many shapes and sizes, each suited to its work. Red blood cells are round discs that slip through narrow vessels. Nerve cells are long and branched to carry messages. Muscle cells are spindle-shaped so that they can contract. The yolk of a hen's egg is a single cell, large enough to see.

Parts of a cell

  • Cell membrane: the thin outer skin of the cell. It holds the cell together and controls what goes in and out.
  • Cytoplasm: the jelly-like material that fills the cell. Smaller working parts, called organelles, float in it, and most of the cell's activities take place here.
  • Nucleus: a round body, usually near the centre. It is the control room of the cell. It contains thread-like chromosomes, which carry genes, the instructions passed from parents to children.
  • Mitochondria: the powerhouses, which release energy from food.
  • Vacuoles: sacs that store water, food or waste.

Plant cell and animal cell

FeaturePlant cellAnimal cell
Cell wallPresent, outside the cell membrane; it gives shape and strengthAbsent
ChloroplastsPresent in green parts; they contain chlorophyll for photosynthesisAbsent
VacuoleOne large central vacuoleSmall vacuoles, or none
ShapeUsually fixed and box-likeUsually rounded or irregular

From cells to a body

In a multicellular body, cells do not work alone. Similar cells doing the same job form a tissue, such as muscle tissue. Different tissues join to form an organ, such as the heart. Organs working together make an organ system, such as the circulatory system. All the systems together make the organism.

Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism

Key terms

Cell
the basic unit of structure and function of living things
Unicellular
made of a single cell
Nucleus
the control centre of the cell
Cytoplasm
the jelly-like material inside the cell
Chloroplast
the green part of a plant cell where photosynthesis happens
Tissue
a group of similar cells doing the same job

Try it yourself

Peel the thin, transparent skin from the inner side of an onion layer and spread it on a glass slide with a drop of water. If your school has a microscope, look at it: you will see neat, brick-like plant cells, each with a wall and a nucleus.

Quick questions

Answer in your head or on paper first, then tap to check.

Who discovered the cell, and when?

Robert Hooke, in 1665.

Which part of the cell is called its control room?

The nucleus.

Name two parts found in plant cells but not in animal cells.

The cell wall and chloroplasts.

Arrange in order: organ, cell, organism, tissue, organ system.

Cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism.
