Newton's laws of motion

Inertia, F = ma, and action and reaction, with everyday examples and a worked sum.

Classes 9 and 10Physics565 words

Why does a passenger fall forward when a bus brakes? Why is it harder to push a loaded cart than an empty one? Why does a gun kick back when fired? In 1687 Sir Isaac Newton answered all such questions with three short laws.

First law: the law of inertia

An object stays at rest, or keeps moving in a straight line at the same speed, unless an unbalanced force acts on it.

Things do not start, stop or turn by themselves. This unwillingness to change is called inertia. The more mass an object has, the greater its inertia: a truck is harder to start and harder to stop than a bicycle.

  • When a bus starts suddenly, your feet move with it but your upper body tends to stay at rest. You fall backward.
  • When it brakes, your body tends to keep moving. You fall forward. This is why we wear seat belts.
  • Flick a card from under a coin placed on a glass: the card flies off and the coin drops into the glass.

A ball rolling on the ground does stop, but only because friction acts on it. On a perfectly smooth surface it would roll on for ever.

Second law: force and acceleration

The momentum of a body is its mass multiplied by its velocity (p = mv). A fast cricket ball has more momentum than a slow one, and a moving truck far more than a moving bicycle.

The rate of change of momentum of a body is proportional to the force applied and takes place in the direction of the force. For a body of constant mass, this gives the famous equation:

Force = mass × acceleration, or F = ma

Force is measured in newtons (N). One newton is the force that gives a mass of 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s2.

Worked example: a force acts on a 5 kg trolley and gives it an acceleration of 2 m/s2. F = ma = 5 × 2 = 10 N.

The law explains much that we see. The same push gives a light object a bigger acceleration than a heavy one. A fielder pulls the hands back while catching a fast ball: this increases the time taken to stop the ball, so the force on the hands is smaller. High jumpers land on thick foam for the same reason.

Third law: action and reaction

To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Forces always come in pairs. If A pushes B, then B pushes A just as hard in the opposite direction. The two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel each other.

  • When we walk, our foot pushes the ground backward; the ground pushes us forward.
  • A swimmer pushes the water back; the water pushes the swimmer ahead.
  • A rocket throws hot gases downward at great speed; the gases push the rocket upward.
  • When a bullet is fired forward, the gun recoils backward. The gun moves much more slowly because its mass is much greater.
  • Step from a small boat to the bank and the boat moves away behind you.

Conservation of momentum

From the second and third laws together comes an important result: when two bodies interact and no outside force acts, their total momentum stays the same. The momentum lost by one is gained by the other. This explains collisions, recoil and rocket flight.

Key terms

Inertia
the tendency of a body to resist a change in its state of rest or motion
Momentum
mass multiplied by velocity
Acceleration
the rate of change of velocity
Newton
the SI unit of force
Recoil
the backward movement of a gun when it is fired

Try it yourself

Place a smooth card on a glass and a coin on the card. Flick the card away sharply with your finger. The coin falls straight into the glass: it stayed where it was because of inertia.

Quick questions

Answer in your head or on paper first, then tap to check.

State Newton's first law of motion.

A body stays at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless an unbalanced force acts on it.

What force is needed to give a 10 kg mass an acceleration of 3 m/s²?

F = ma = 10 × 3 = 30 N.

Why does a gun recoil when fired?

The gun pushes the bullet forward and the bullet pushes the gun back with an equal force (third law).

Why do passengers fall forward when a bus stops suddenly?

Because of inertia, their bodies tend to keep moving forward.
