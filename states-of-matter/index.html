States of matter: solid, liquid and gas

Why ice is hard, water flows and steam spreads, explained through tiny particles.

Classes 3 to 5Chemistry361 words

Everything around you that has weight and takes up space is called matter. Your book, the water in your bottle and the air in a balloon are all matter. Matter is found mainly in three states: solid, liquid and gas.

Made of tiny particles

All matter is made of extremely small particles, far too small to see. The state of a substance depends on how these particles are packed and how they move.

Solids

In a solid, the particles are packed very closely in a fixed pattern. They cannot move from their places; they only vibrate. That is why a solid has a fixed shape and a fixed volume. A stone stays the same shape wherever you keep it. Examples: wood, iron, ice, a pencil.

Liquids

In a liquid, the particles are close together but not fixed. They can slide past one another. So a liquid has a fixed volume but no fixed shape: it takes the shape of its container, and it can flow. One glass of water poured into a bowl is still the same amount of water. Examples: water, milk, oil.

Gases

In a gas, the particles are far apart and move freely and fast in all directions. A gas has no fixed shape and no fixed volume. It spreads out to fill any container, and it can be squeezed into a smaller space, as when air is pumped into a tyre. Examples: air, oxygen, steam, cooking gas.

Changing from one state to another

Heating gives particles more energy, so they move faster and break free. Cooling does the opposite.

  • Melting: solid to liquid. Ice melts into water at 0°C.
  • Evaporation and boiling: liquid to gas. Water boils into steam at 100°C.
  • Condensation: gas to liquid. Steam touching a cold lid turns into drops of water.
  • Freezing: liquid to solid. Water kept in the freezer becomes ice.

Water is special because we meet it in all three states in daily life: ice, water and steam. In each state it is still the same substance. Only the arrangement and movement of its particles have changed. Such a change, which can be reversed, is called a physical change.

Key terms

Matter
anything that has mass and takes up space
Volume
the amount of space a thing takes up
Melting
solid changing to liquid
Freezing
liquid changing to solid
Condensation
gas changing to liquid

Try it yourself

Take an ice cube in a steel bowl. Watch it melt (solid to liquid). Ask an adult to heat the water gently and hold a cool steel plate above it: the steam turns back into drops on the plate (gas to liquid). You have seen three states and two changes.

Quick questions

Answer in your head or on paper first, then tap to check.

Which state of matter has a fixed shape and a fixed volume?

Solid.

Why does a liquid take the shape of its container?

Its particles are not fixed in place and can slide past one another.

What is the change from liquid to solid called?

Freezing.

At what temperature does water boil?

At 100°C, at sea level.
